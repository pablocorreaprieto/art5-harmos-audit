To the Editor-in-Chief of *Education and Information Technologies*

Dear Dr Keane,

Please find attached the manuscript "Swiss grade labels and the lexical demand of French reading texts written by large language models: A preregistered audit", which I submit for consideration as an original research article.

Teachers are advised to name their pupils' grade when they ask a language model for a reading text, but grade labels are local: the labels of French-speaking Switzerland (« 5H », « 5P ») name pupils two years younger than the fifth year of primary school in France. In a preregistered audit of 7,680 French reading texts written by six large language models, the bare labels gave texts close to the lexical demand of the matching age on average. Exploratory analyses showed that the models departed from their own age-only texts in both directions, one lighter model by about two years, and that the model that wrote a text mattered more for its vocabulary than the wording of the grade. The article gives teachers and tool developers a simple rule, state the age and read the text, and shows why evaluations of AI tools for teachers should report results model by model.

The study follows the recommendation of a review of language models in education (Yan et al., 2024), that studies report the exact models, prompts and temperature they used: every prompt, parameter and raw output is archived, and the results are reported for each model as well as pooled. It involved no human participants, and its data are texts generated through the providers' APIs. The hypotheses, measure, exclusion rules, sample size and analyses were preregistered before data collection. Because the deposited preregistration contains the address of the study repository, which names me, I give its link on the title page for the editor only, and an anonymised copy of the repository that includes the preregistration text is available for reviewers.

I conceived the study and designed the protocol for AI-assisted research under which an AI assistant carried it out. The manuscript declares in full what the assistant did, how its work was checked, which separate AI sessions reviewed it, and how the Anthropic API calls were paid, mostly with the API credits included in my paid Claude Max subscription. Anthropic also made two of the audited models and the assistant. As Springer's policy asks, this use is documented in the Methods as well as in the Statements and Declarations. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published.
