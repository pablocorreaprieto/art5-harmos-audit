To the Editors of the *British Journal of Educational Technology*

Dear Editors,

Please find attached the manuscript "TITLE", which I submit for consideration as an original research article.

Teachers are advised to name their pupils' grade when they ask a language model for a reading text, but grade labels are local, and the labels of French-speaking Switzerland (« 5H », « 5P ») name pupils two years younger than a fifth grade in France. In a preregistered audit of {{n_texts}} French reading texts written by {{n_models_word}} large language models, I find that the bare labels give texts close to the lexical demand of the matching age on average, that two lighter models misread them, one by about two years, and that adding the age to the label narrows the differences between models. The article gives teachers and tool developers a simple rule, and it shows why evaluations of AI tools for teachers should report results model by model.

The study fits the journal's interest in artificial intelligence in education and in its consequences for practice, and it adds to the journal's recent work on the readability of AI-generated material for primary pupils, such as the study by Li, Xing, Li, Zhu and Oh (2024) on generated mathematics stories. It involved no human participants: its data are texts generated through the providers' APIs. The hypotheses, measure, exclusion rules, sample size and analyses were preregistered before data collection, and the prompts, raw outputs, code and every number of the article are in a repository, given anonymised for review and to be archived with a DOI on acceptance. The use of generative AI in the study is declared in full in the manuscript.
