To the Editor-in-Chief of *Education and Information Technologies*

Dear Dr Keane,

Please find attached the manuscript "{{ms_title}}", which I submit for consideration as an original research article.

Teachers are advised to name their pupils' grade when they ask a language model for a reading text, but grade labels are local: the labels of French-speaking Switzerland (« 5H », « 5P ») name pupils two years younger than the fifth year of primary school in France. In a preregistered audit of {{n_texts}} French reading texts written by {{n_models_word}} large language models, the bare labels gave texts close, on average, to the lexical demand that the same model gives the matching age. Exploratory analyses showed that the models departed from their own age-only texts in both directions, one lighter model by about two years, and that at the same requested age the models' texts differed about as much as one model's texts across the primary cycle. The article shows teachers and tool developers that adding the age to a grade label is a cheap precaution but no substitute for reading the text, and why evaluations of AI tools for teachers should report results model by model. Its method, comparing each label with the same model's texts for the matching age, applies to any school system whose grade labels may be read in another system's terms. I am a primary teacher in the canton of Vaud, where the question arose.

The study follows the recommendation of a review of language models in education (Yan et al., 2024), that studies report the exact models, prompts and temperature they used: every prompt, parameter and raw output is archived, and the results are reported for each model as well as pooled. It involved no human participants, and its data are texts generated through the providers' APIs. The hypotheses, measure, exclusion rules, sample size and analyses were preregistered before data collection. Because the deposited preregistration contains the address of the study repository, which names me, I give its link on the title page for the editor only. An anonymised copy of the repository, which includes the preregistration text, is uploaded with the submission for the reviewers.

I chose the topic and set the main rules of the protocol for AI-assisted research under which the study was carried out, with two decision points reserved for me. Under it an AI assistant designed the study in detail, wrote the code, ran the analyses and drafted the manuscript, except the advice to teachers, which I wrote. The manuscript declares in full what the assistant did, how its work was checked, which separate AI sessions reviewed it, and how the Anthropic API calls were paid, with promotional credits that Anthropic granted to my API account. Anthropic also made two of the audited models and the assistant. As the journal's guidelines ask, this use is documented in the Methods as well as in the Statements and Declarations, and I take full responsibility for the work. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published.
